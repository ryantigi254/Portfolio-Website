Diagnostic Subpage · Meta-metric

Controllability

Does behaviour move in the intended direction when an explicit control is changed?

Design

Same-case, three-arm comparison

Coverage

Five control-conditioned study suites

Status

Experimental diagnostic, not a safety card

What is being tested

Controllability is a meta-metric over the base studies. It compares behaviour with a control absent and present, then measures the paired change at the same evaluation unit. The result asks whether a specified intervention has an interpretable effect; it does not replace the benchmark’s base faithfulness, pressure, or continuity metrics.

Why investigate controllability

Trustworthy evaluation is not only about detecting a failure; it is also about seeing whether the relevant behaviour can be moved deliberately. If a pressure-resistance, reasoning, or continuity metric responds predictably to a clear intervention, it creates a concrete target for improvement. That target could later be explored through supervised fine-tuning, preference optimisation or RLHF, reinforcement learning, or a more constrained control mechanism. This benchmark measures the response to a control; it does not claim that any of those interventions has been trained or validated here.

The design is informed by Chen et al., Reasoning Models Struggle to Control their Chains of Thought, which motivates checking whether an apparent control is genuine rather than merely prompt-shaped behaviour.

Data coverage

The controllability-specific composition plot describes the category spread of the control-knob evaluation surface. It is coverage, not performance.

Reading the plot: the radar shows which diagnostic categories occur in the controllability splits for the overall set and each study slice. It is the controllability coverage plot formerly shown on the main benchmark page.

The Working Draft 8 heatmap then records endpoint availability. It preserves successful, missing, and non-measurable model–study combinations rather than inferring results for them.

Reading the plot: green OK cells are recorded endpoints; M marks missing coverage and NM marks a non-measurable comparison. The controllability rows cover Study A, Study A Bias, Study B, Study B Multi-turn, and Study C.

Study-level result plot

The following recorded Study B plot measures the explicit-control effect using the turn-of-flip proxy. It is secondary diagnostic evidence, not a global model ranking or a replacement for the released Study B result.

Reading the plot: bars show the delta in turn-of-flip proxy by recorded model; error bars show the reported uncertainty. The dashed line is the project’s stated 0.5-turn minimum meaningful delta.

Interpretation boundary

Controllability roll-ups are experimental summaries. They require matched cases, paired bootstrap confidence intervals, and study-specific interpretation; they do not establish clinical safety or deployment readiness.
